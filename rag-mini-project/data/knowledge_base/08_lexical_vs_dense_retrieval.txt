LEXICAL AND DENSE RETRIEVAL
===========================

Lexical retrieval
-----------------
Lexical retrieval scores documents by the words they share with the query. BM25, summarised by Robertson and Zaragoza in 2009, is the standard function; common default values are k1 of about 1.2 and b of about 0.75. Lexical retrieval is excellent for exact names, codes and rare terms, but it fails when the query uses different words than the document, which is called vocabulary mismatch.

Dense retrieval
---------------
Dense retrieval compares embedding vectors and can match a paraphrase that shares no words with the passage. It can, however, rank a passage that is only topically related above a passage with the exact answer, and it is weaker on rare identifiers.

Hybrid retrieval
----------------
Hybrid retrieval combines both signals. Reciprocal rank fusion, proposed by Cormack and colleagues in 2009, adds 1 divided by (k plus rank) for every ranked list a document appears in, with k commonly set to 60. Because it uses ranks instead of raw scores, it avoids having to calibrate lexical and dense scores against each other.

Practical advice
----------------
Start with a single retriever, build a labelled question set, measure Precision@k, Recall@k and MRR, and only add complexity such as hybrid search or re-ranking when the measurements show a clear weakness.
